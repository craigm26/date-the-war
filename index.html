    What this page is for

    A world war can begin gradually and get its start date afterward. Nobody on 18 September 1931 knew they were in one; some historians now say they were. This page does not declare a third world war. It puts every sourced event since 1900 on one globe, lets you narrow the window to a day, a month, or a year, and lets you play the record forward. Date it from wherever you like and see what that dating makes you include.

    Why wars happen, according to the field

    Political science separates the things states fight over, such as territory, security, resources and identity, from the mechanisms that turn a dispute into violence. The starting point is a puzzle: war destroys what both sides want, so a settlement both prefer to fighting should nearly always exist. The mechanisms below are the reasons states fail to find it, accept it, or keep it. Each is tagged with the level it lives at: the leader, the state, or the system.

      What "world war" would have to mean

      • International armed conflict is a legal status. Armed force between states establishes it. No declaration is needed. The United States has been in one with Iran since February.
      • War in the research datasets is a count. The Uppsala program calls a conflict a war in any year with at least 1,000 battle deaths. Thirteen conflicts cleared that bar in 2025, the most since 1992.
      • World war is a historical label with no agreed threshold. The usual meaning is a general war among most of the major powers. The two previous ones were also marked by whole economies reorganized for the fight. Neither dataset above has a category for it.

      The test that fits the label is one question: are at least two major powers shooting at each other directly, in more than one theater? On the record here the present reads as linked wars, not a general one. The broadest alternative, which dates a single global war from the early 2010s, also swallows most of the Cold War, and a label that cannot tell those apart is not doing work.

      Where the author stands, as of 8 September 2026

      The United States is at war with Iran. It is not in a world war on the conventional meaning, and there is no accepted list of requirements that can be declared met. The conflicts are more connected than at any point since 1945, and they are colliding without converging: the participants still want different things. What would change this reading is sustained direct combat between major powers spreading across linked theaters, with alliances pulling in more of them. That could justify the label before any nuclear use or any announcement, and a later historian might then date the war from a regional fight that had already been running for years.
